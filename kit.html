What goes into a system

The four components every system needs

Whether you buy a bundled kit or source your own, every plug-in solar setup has the same four pieces. Here's what each one does.

☀️
Solar Panels
Typically 250W–400W per panel

250W panels are easier to handle, ship cheaper, and are a good starting point. 400W panels generate more per unit but are larger and heavier. Panel prices vary significantly — check current market pricing before buying, as this is one of the most volatile cost inputs in any system.

Price varies — check current listings before assuming any figure
⚡
Microinverter
Typically $150–$300; UL 1741 certified

Converts DC power from your panels into AC electricity your home can use. The APsystems EZ1-M is the most widely used US model (~$150–$180). The EcoFlow STREAM is a newer alternative at ~$299 that integrates with EcoFlow's battery ecosystem. Look for UL 1741 certification and built-in WiFi monitoring.

Est. cost: $150–$300 depending on model
🔗
MC4 DC Cables
10 AWG, weatherproof, pre-terminated

The cables that connect your panels to the microinverter. Pre-terminated with MC4 connectors — no splicing required, just click them in. Rated for outdoor UV exposure.

Est. cost: $20–$30
🔌
Feed-In Safety Plug Cord
NEMA 5-15, with safety interlock

The cord that connects the microinverter output to your wall outlet. Uses a safety plug design with a physical interlock that prevents the cord from being live when unplugged. Required by law in several states.

Est. cost: $25–$40

How to buy

Bundled kit vs. sourcing your own

Two approaches, each with real tradeoffs. Neither is obviously better for everyone.

✅ Bundled kit (e.g. PluggedSolar, Bright Saver)

  • Everything arrives together, designed to work as a system
  • Less research required — components are already matched
  • Usually includes some form of customer support
  • You know the total cost upfront, including shipping
  • Higher per-watt cost than self-sourcing in most cases

🟡 Self-sourced components

  • Potentially lower cost, especially if you find panels secondhand
  • More flexibility — choose panel size based on your space
  • Requires more research to ensure component compatibility
  • Shipping panels separately adds cost and complexity
  • No bundled support if something doesn't work
